# Hakim Medicine — Herb & Simple Ingredient Reference

This file provides extended monographs for single-ingredient simples used in classical
hakim (Unani) medicine. Each entry follows the format established in Ibn Sina's
*Al-Qanun fi al-Tibb* and Al-Biruni's *Kitab al-Saydana*.

---

## How to Use This File

Load this reference when:
- The user asks for detailed monograph information on a specific herb or simple
- A remedy entry in the main catalog requires classical source qualification
- Dosage, temperament, or combination rules need elaboration

## Herbs: A

### Aconite (Monkshood) | Bish / Atees | بیش / اتیس
**Latin**: *Aconitum napellus* / *Aconitum heterophyllum*
**Temperament**: Hot 4°, Dry 4° (raw); after shodhana/islah: Hot 2°, Dry 2°
**Parts Used**: Root
**Classical Sources**: Ibn Sina (*Qanun*), Al-Zahrawi, Dioscorides (Arabic trans.)
**Preparation Methods**:
- *Detoxification (Islah)*: Boil root in cow's milk 3× changes, or soak in vinegar 24 h,
  then dry — classical requirement before any internal use
- Powder (post-islah), pill, electuary
**Classical Uses**:
- Joint pain, paralysis, nerve pain (topical liniment)
- Intermittent fevers (small oral dose, post-islah only)
- Antidote base for scorpion sting (classically combined with theriac)
**Dosage**: Post-islah powder: 1–3 ratti (125–375 mg) oral; topical: as needed
**Combination Rules**: Combined with honey, dry ginger, or long pepper to moderate cold
**Cautions**: ⚠️ HIGHLY TOXIC raw. Classical texts mandate islah (detoxification) before
any internal use. Overdose causes cardiac arrest. Al-Zahrawi notes antidote is warm
clarified butter, olive oil, or theriac magna.

---

### Aloe | Sabr | صبر
**Latin**: *Aloe vera*, *Aloe socotrina* (Socotrine aloe — prized)
**Temperament**: Hot 1°, Dry 3°
**Parts Used**: Dried latex (bitter resin), gel
**Classical Sources**: Ibn Sina, Dioscorides, Galen (Arabic translation)
**Preparation Methods**:
- Powder, pill (hab), dissolved in water or rose water
- Mixed into electuary (ma'jun) with honey
**Classical Uses**:
- Purgative (bitter latex): liver congestion, melancholic humors, constipation
- Wound healing (gel applied topically)
- Eye disorders (diluted wash)
- Expels excess bile (safra)
**Dosage**: Purgative: 3–6 g powder in warm water; topical: as needed
**Combination Rules**: Paired with mastic (mastagi) to protect stomach; with rose water
to reduce irritation
**Cautions**: Avoid in pregnancy (uterine stimulant); avoid in hemorrhoids; avoid with
cold, moist constitution without warming adjuvants

---

### Anise | Anisoon | انیسون
**Latin**: *Pimpinella anisum*
**Temperament**: Hot 3°, Dry 1°
**Parts Used**: Seed
**Classical Sources**: Ibn Sina, Ibn Baytar
**Preparation Methods**:
- Decoction, infusion, distillate (arq)
- Powder in honey or syrup
**Classical Uses**:
- Carminative: expels wind, relieves colic
- Respiratory: loosens phlegm, eases cough
- Galactagogue: increases breast milk
- Diuretic, mild
**Dosage**: 3–6 g seed decoction; arq (distillate) 30–60 ml
**Combination Rules**: Combines well with fennel and caraway for digestive complaints
**Cautions**: Generally safe; avoid excessive doses in pregnancy

## Herbs: B

### Black Seed (Nigella) | Habbatus Sawda / Kalonji | حبة السوداء / کلونجی
**Latin**: *Nigella sativa*
**Temperament**: Hot 3°, Dry 3°
**Parts Used**: Seed, fixed oil
**Classical Sources**: Ibn Sina (*Qanun* — "cures all diseases except death"),
Hadith medicine, Al-Biruni
**Preparation Methods**:
- Seed: chewed raw, powdered in honey, decoction, mixed with vinegar
- Oil: expressed cold, used internally and topically
- Fumigation: burned seeds inhaled for headache, nasal congestion
**Classical Uses**:
- General tonic and immune strengthener
- Digestive: carminative, anthelmintic (expels worms)
- Respiratory: asthma, cough, bronchitis (seed + honey + warm water)
- Topical: skin diseases, alopecia (oil applied), vitiligo (combined with vinegar)
- Fever, jaundice, dropsy
**Dosage**: Seed powder 1–3 g; oil 2–5 ml; 2–3× daily
**Combination Rules**: With honey universally; with garlic for infections; with
vinegar for skin; with pomegranate juice for worms
**Cautions**: Avoid high doses in pregnancy (uterine stimulant in large amounts);
moderate use considered safe in classical texts

---

### Black Pepper | Filfil Siyah | فلفل سیاہ
**Latin**: *Piper nigrum*
**Temperament**: Hot 4°, Dry 3°
**Parts Used**: Fruit (dried)
**Classical Sources**: Dioscorides, Ibn Sina, Ibn Baytar
**Preparation Methods**:
- Whole, crushed, powdered
- In electuary, pill, decoction
**Classical Uses**:
- Digestive: stimulates appetite, carminative
- Adjuvant (*musahhil*): enhances absorption of other remedies
- Antidote: component of theriac preparations
- Respiratory: loosens thick phlegm
- Joint pain: topical paste
**Dosage**: 0.5–2 g powder
**Combination Rules**: Universally used as adjuvant; paired with long pepper and dry
ginger in *trikatu*-equivalent formulas
**Cautions**: Avoid in inflammatory conditions, hot-temperament patients, gastric ulcer

## Herbs: C

### Camphor | Kafur | کافور
**Latin**: *Cinnamomum camphora*
**Temperament**: Cold 4°, Dry 2° (one of the coldest simples in classical Unani)
**Parts Used**: Crystalline sublimate from wood
**Classical Sources**: Ibn Sina, Serapion
**Preparation Methods**:
- Dissolved in rose water or alcohol base
- Mixed into cooling electuaries, pills
- Topical paste, liniment
**Classical Uses**:
- High fever with delirium (internal, very small dose)
- Cooling cardiac tonic: palpitations from heat (combined with rose, sandalwood)
- Topical analgesic: headache, joint inflammation
- Fumigation: respiratory infections
**Dosage**: Oral: 0.1–0.3 g maximum; topical: as needed
**Combination Rules**: Combined with rose water, sandalwood, and violet in cooling
formulas; with musk in cardiac preparations
**Cautions**: ⚠️ Toxic in doses above 0.5 g oral. Classical texts warn of convulsions
and respiratory depression with overdose. Contraindicated in cold-temperament patients.

## Herbs: G

### Garlic | Thoom | ثوم
**Latin**: *Allium sativum*
**Temperament**: Hot 4°, Dry 4°
**Parts Used**: Bulb
**Classical Sources**: Dioscorides, Ibn Sina, Galen
**Preparation Methods**:
- Raw, roasted, boiled, vinegar macerate, oil infusion, poultice
**Classical Uses**:
- Expectorant and bronchial opener
- Anthelmintic: expels intestinal parasites
- Antidote to food poisoning and animal bites
- Diuretic
- Topical: alopecia (rubbed on scalp), infected wounds, snake/insect bites
**Dosage**: 1–3 cloves raw; 5–10 ml garlic oil; topical as needed
**Combination Rules**: With honey for infections; with vinegar for parasites;
moderated by yogurt or milk in hot-constitution patients
**Cautions**: Contraindicated in hot-temperament; may aggravate hemorrhoids and
gastric inflammation; avoid in eye disorders per Ibn Sina

## Minerals & Metals

### Mercury (Processed) | Paara / Zibaq | پارہ / زیبق
**Classical Form**: *Kajjali* (black sulfide of mercury, classically processed)
**Temperament**: Cold 4°, Moist (processed form varies)
**Preparation / Purification (Shodhana/Islah)**:
- Raw mercury must never be used internally
- Classical process: triturate with sulfur to produce mercuric sulfide (kajjali);
  or incinerate repeatedly with sulfur, herbs, and adjuvants until powder no longer
  stains copper
- Used in compound formulas only after complete purification cycles
**Classical Uses** (compound formulas only):
- Syphilis (atar al-ras): classical primary treatment
- Chronic skin diseases: psoriasis, leprosy-type conditions
- Deep joint disease
**Dosage**: As directed in specific compound formulas; typically 1/4–1/2 ratti
(30–60 mg kajjali) in compound only
**Cautions**: ⚠️⚠️ HIGHLY TOXIC. Classical texts describe elaborate purification
mandatory before use. Al-Zahrawi and Ibn Sina both warn of serious harm from
improper preparation. Modern context: mercuric compounds are acutely toxic;
this entry is historical documentation only.

---

### Sulfur | Kibrit | کبریت
**Temperament**: Hot 2°–3°, Dry 2°
**Parts Used**: Purified native sulfur
**Preparation**: Washed and purified; never used raw internally in large quantities
**Classical Uses**:
- Skin diseases: scabies, mange, fungal conditions (topical ointment with lard or oil)
- Respiratory: fumigation for bronchitis, asthma
- Anthelmintic: small oral dose in electuary
**Dosage**: Topical: 10–20% in ointment base; oral (historical): 0.5–2 g in electuary
**Combination Rules**: With clarified butter or lard for topical; with honey orally
**Cautions**: Avoid contact with eyes; large oral doses historically caused GI irritation

## Resins & Gums

### Mastic | Mastagi Rumi | مصطکی رومی
**Latin**: *Pistacia lentiscus*
**Temperament**: Hot 1°, Dry 2°
**Parts Used**: Resin
**Classical Sources**: Dioscorides, Ibn Sina, Al-Biruni
**Preparation Methods**: Chewed, dissolved in warm water, incorporated into
electuaries and pills
**Classical Uses**:
- Gastric protectant: used with purgatives to prevent gastric harm
- Gastric ulcer, heartburn
- Oral hygiene: gum disease, halitosis
- Mild astringent for diarrhea
- Wound healing: incorporated in plasters
**Dosage**: 1–3 g; chewed or dissolved
**Combination Rules**: Standard adjuvant with aloe and other purgatives to protect stomach
**Cautions**: Generally well tolerated; rare allergy

## Animal-Derived Simples

### Castoreum | Jundbidastar | جند بیدستر
**Source**: Castor glands of *Castor fiber* (European beaver)
**Temperament**: Hot 3°, Dry 3°
**Classical Sources**: Dioscorides, Ibn Sina, Galen
**Preparation**: Dried gland, powdered; dissolved in wine (historically) or rose water
**Classical Uses**:
- Antispasmodic: epilepsy, hysteria, uterine spasms
- Nerve tonic: paralysis, numbness
- Antidote component in theriac formulas
- Menstrual regulation (emmenagogue)
**Dosage**: 0.5–2 g powder; 2× daily
**Combination Rules**: Combined with asafoetida and valerian in antispasmodic formulas
**Cautions**: Avoid in pregnancy (uterine stimulant); avoid with hot constitution
