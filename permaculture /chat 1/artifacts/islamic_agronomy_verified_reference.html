2. Catalog Scope & Limitations

What the catalog captures: 248 plant entries with agricultural roles, Arabic names, and scientific identifications — representing plants for which a cultivation note, soil interaction, or relationship can be stated. Covers all major categories across the three core texts.
What the catalog does not capture: Ibn al-Awwam alone lists 50+ grapevine cultivars, 30+ olive cultivars, and numerous wheat varieties as separate named entries. Including all variety-level entries would push the count to 600+. The catalog here operates at species/group level, not variety level.
Scientific name precision: Most identifications are reliable. Flags are applied where Arabic regional names may map to a different species than the European equivalent (e.g., some Allium identifications, some Artemisia sp.). Rosa damascena is the conventional identification for Ibn al-Awwam's ward al-jūrī but some scholars argue for Rosa centifolia.
Source separation: Ibn al-Baytar and Ibn Sina are cited for plant identification only, not as cultivation sources. Attributing cultivation advice to them would be an error.

12. Corrections to Earlier Outputs

Errors introduced during this session and their corrections.

Error 1 — Modern companion planting imported as Islamic text: Rosemary, Sage, Hyssop, Southernwood, Lemon balm as vine companions; Catmint and Mint with eggplant; Summer savory with broad bean; Anise with coriander (companion pair). These pairing patterns are real in Western herbalism and permaculture but are not found in the muwāfaqa / munāfara sections of Ibn al-Awwam. Removed from "Explicit" and from the network.
Error 2 — Rotation mislabeled as companion planting: Broad bean → Wheat, Lupin → Barley, etc. were labeled "Friendship" in the network. They are temporal rotation sequences — the crops do not grow simultaneously. Recategorized as "Rotation" throughout.
Error 3 — Pest-control practices labeled as biological friendships: Pennyroyal / Wormwood protecting stored grain, Oleander fumigating olive trees, Squill as rodent bait — these are farmer practices, not plant-to-plant ecological relationships. Recategorized as "Pest control (human-mediated)" and separated from companion planting in all tables and the network.
Error 4 — Chamomile as soil restorer: Chamomile is a soil indicator in Ibn al-Awwam (grows on disturbed ground), not a soil restorer. Removed from the restoration category.
Error 5 — Colocynth "friendship" with wheat: Colocynth's roots are toxic to wheat (hostility, per Ibn Wahshiyya). Its fruit is used as a rodent bait (pest control practice). These two facts were conflated into a "friendship" link. Corrected: colocynth → wheat remains Hostility; bait use is listed under Pest Control.
Error 6 — Over-specific windbreak pairings: Cypress → Olive, Cypress → Grapevine, Cypress → Wheat listed as explicit. Actual text: cypress described as general windbreak. These specific pairings are modern reconstruction. Corrected above.
Error 7 — Scale miscommunication: Catalog stated "248 plants" without clarifying this is species-level coverage, not variety-level. Ibn al-Awwam alone has 500–600 entries when varieties are counted. Scope statement added to Section 2.
Confirmed accurate: All walnut allelopathy links (root secretions, named targets). All fennel hostility links (coriander, cumin; carrot strongly implied). Cabbage harms vine (explicit). All grafting relationships. All soil restoration legumes. Garlic as base companion to rose, pomegranate, almond, olive, vine. Rue as base companion to olive and fig. Vine / fig / olive as orchard companions. All caprification notes. Hand pollination of date palm. All pest-control plant identifications and their agricultural targets.
